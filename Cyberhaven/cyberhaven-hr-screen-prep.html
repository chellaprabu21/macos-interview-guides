Know the company in 60 seconds

You only need enough to sound genuinely curious, not encyclopedic.

  • What they do: Data Detection & Response — they trace data lineage (where a file came from, every hop it took) instead of pattern-matching content like legacy DLP. That lineage drives classification and policy.
  • The macOS role: the endpoint sensor that watches every data movement across apps and files — hundreds of thousands of events, near-zero performance impact, hardened against attack.
  • Stage: ~$250M raised (Khosla, Redpoint). Leadership pedigree from CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, Meta, Google. Growth-stage, so expect "early contributor, shape the architecture" framing — mirror that language back.
  • Team shape: distributed across CET and US-Eastern. India is an approved location — your evening availability from Bengaluru is a selling point, say so unprompted.

Your 60-second pitch

"I've spent ten years building native macOS client software for security products — Zoho, Citrix, and now JumpCloud, where I'm a Staff engineer tech-leading the Endpoint AI Governance team. My day job is essentially the job in your JD: a fleet-scale macOS agent built on System Extensions and the Endpoint Security framework, deployed across millions of endpoints, where performance and reliability are non-negotiable because we sit in the user's critical path. I've done the hard parts of this domain — multithreaded event processing, Instruments-driven profiling, secure-by-design client code handling untrusted data. What pulls me to Cyberhaven is that the sensor is the product. Data lineage on the endpoint is the most interesting unsolved problem in DLP, and I want to build it early enough to shape the architecture."

Questions she will actually ask

HR screens are logistics + motivation + red-flag checks. Have crisp numbers ready.

Why are you looking to leave JumpCloud?

Never negative. Pull, not push: "I'm not running from anything — JumpCloud has been great. But at Cyberhaven the endpoint sensor is the core product, not a supporting component of an identity platform. I want my macOS depth to be the company's main event, at a stage where I can still shape architecture."

What are your compensation expectations?

Deflect once, gracefully: "I'd rather first confirm mutual fit — but I'm benchmarking against Staff-level offers in the Bengaluru security market, and I trust Cyberhaven's band is competitive for that. Can you share the range for this role?" Many recruiters will share the band if you ask directly.

If pressed for a number, give a range anchored to your Staff-level benchmarks (you've already researched Okta/Zscaler/India Staff bands) — not your current CTC.

Do not volunteer current CTC. If asked, it's fine in India hiring culture to redirect to expectations instead.
Notice period / earliest start date?

State it plainly with the buyout/negotiation option if one exists. Startups move fast; a shorter effective notice is a real advantage — mention it if you can offer one.

Are you comfortable with CET / US-Eastern collaboration?

"Yes — I already work with distributed teams. CET overlap is comfortable from IST afternoons, and I can hold a regular evening window for US-East syncs." Concrete, committed, done.

Tell me about your current role

Compressed version of the pitch: Staff engineer, tech lead, fleet-scale macOS security agent, ES framework + System Extensions, device trust and conditional access. One breath, then stop. Let her ask follow-ups.

The one honest wrinkle: Senior vs Staff

This role is titled Senior; you are a Staff engineer and tech lead. That's a real level question — don't pretend it isn't, and don't let it fester until the offer stage.

  • Raise it lightly in this call: "I noticed the posting is Senior-level — I'm currently Staff and tech-leading a team. Is there flexibility on leveling for strong candidates, or is the band fixed?"
  • Startups often flex titles and comp for a candidate who exceeds the JD — but only if you ask early.
  • If leveling is truly fixed, decide consciously whether scope + equity + product-fit outweigh the title step-down (you already assessed Omnissa as a step-down and passed — apply the same bar).

Ask her these (pick 2–3)

  • "What does the rest of the interview loop look like, and what's the timeline?" — always ask; shows momentum.
  • "How large is the macOS endpoint team today, and where does this hire fit?"
  • "Is the India team new? How is engineering split across Romania, Poland, and India?"
  • "What does success look like in the first six months for this role?"

Final reminders

  • Commit first, then caveat. Your known failure mode is hedging. For a 30-min HR screen: short declarative answers, zero qualifiers, stop talking when done.
  • It's a vibe check, not a technical round. She's screening for communication, motivation, logistics, and enthusiasm. Warm, energetic, concise wins.
  • Name the referral. "Mann spoke highly of the team" — referrals convert; remind her the signal exists.
  • Book the slot today via the Ashby link, and skim their blog + LinkedIn Life page for 10 minutes before the call so you can reference one specific post.
